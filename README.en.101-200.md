## 101. A Perfect Life Podcast with Dean Jackson and Richard Rossi -  Bonus Episode - I Love Marketing With Joe Polish And Dean Jackson

Published: 2013-02-04 · [Listen to the original audio](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILoveMarketing101.1.mp3?dest-id=200948)

This bonus episode launches a spinoff show called A Perfect Life, where Dean Jackson joins longtime friend Richard Rossi to explore lifestyle design rather than marketing tactics. They agree the purpose of life is happiness, achieved by consciously designing your days around what you love and are good at, rather than sleepwalking through default choices like career or parenthood. Central to their approach is eliminating small daily annoyances they call tolerations, pursuing a frictionless life. Richard shares stories from his Secret Service advance work, including a short pause before acting to check for mistakes, and both describe travel hacks like shipping luggage ahead and tipping handlers to skip lines.

**Key takeaways:**

- Consciously designing your life around what makes you happy, rather than defaulting to what everyone else does, is the core idea behind pursuing a perfect life.
- Identifying and removing small recurring annoyances, called tolerations, gradually compounds into a noticeably happier and more frictionless daily experience.
- Simple techniques, like pausing briefly before an action to check for mistakes or paying a small tip to skip a line, can meaningfully reduce daily stress at low cost.

## 103. The One About Even More Branding - I Love Marketing With Joe Polish And Dean Jackson - Episode#102

Published: 2013-02-13 · [Listen to the original audio](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILoveMarketing102.mp3?dest-id=200948)

Joe Polish and Dean Jackson continue their branding talk, then break down elements of a direct response advertisement: headline, copy building interest and desire, a specific offer, and a deadline. Joe reads old marketing material explaining why image advertising cannot be tracked and why agencies favor creative, award-winning campaigns over ones proven to generate sales. Dean explains it matters more to know prospects individually than for everyone to know your name, illustrated by choosing public recognition versus a list of soon-to-sell homeowners. They cover turning invisible prospects visible through cheap offers, why specific offers beat vague invitations, and how deadlines, like a trial period ending, motivate action over delay.

**Key takeaways:**

- A direct response advertisement needs a strong headline, real interest and desire in the copy, a specific offer, and a deadline in order to reliably generate sales.
- Knowing exactly who your likely buyers are and speaking directly to them is more valuable than broad name recognition among people who will never buy.
- Adding genuine urgency, such as a clear deadline or limited availability, meaningfully increases how many people take action on an offer instead of putting it off.

## 105. The One About Ninja Email Strategies - I Love Marketing With Joe polish and Dean Jackson - Episode # 104

Published: 2013-02-25 · [Listen to the original audio](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILoveMarketing104.mp3?dest-id=200948)

With no outside guest, Dean Jackson leads a deep dive into email marketing while Joe Polish shares a story about sending a very short email that got billionaires Richard Branson and Peter Diamandis to reply within an hour. Dean breaks down the psychology behind why people open, read, or respond to a message, arguing the most effective emails are short, personal, and written to prompt a single reply, treated as one-to-one dialogue rather than a broadcast. He walks through real estate email examples, showing how a plain subject line and a simple sorting question can turn a mass newsletter into individual conversations and dramatically raise click-through and response rates.

**Key takeaways:**

- Emails that are short, personal, and written to invite one specific reply consistently outperform polished broadcast-style messages.
- A subject line's only job is getting the email opened, and the body should aim for exactly one action, either a reply or a single click.
- Asking a simple sorting question, such as which of two options someone wants, can turn a generic list email into an engaged one-to-one conversation.

## 106. The One About Even More Email Marketing - I Love Marketing With Joe Polish And Dean Jackson - Episode #105

Published: 2013-03-05 · [Listen to the original audio](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILoveMarketing105.mp3?dest-id=200948)

With no outside guest, Dean Jackson and Joe Polish continue their series on email marketing, focused on how long leads actually take to convert. Dean cites data showing many prospects buy only after ninety days or more, so businesses should first identify the small share ready to act now instead of chasing everyone equally. He describes sorting opted-in leads by whether they will buy soon and whether they engage in dialogue, and stresses imagining each recipient as one real person rather than writing to a crowd. Joe adds stories about long-running follow-up sequences and warns that acting desperate for a sale repels prospects much like it repels a potential date.

**Key takeaways:**

- Most leads take far longer than a week or two to convert, so a follow-up system needs to reach well past the first ninety days.
- Sort new leads by whether they respond and show readiness to buy soon, then focus early effort only on that smaller, ready group.
- Writing emails as though speaking to one real person, and staying patient rather than sounding desperate, produces far stronger response rates.

She explains how she systemizes every business using written standard operating procedures and strict project task lists with fixed due dates, freeing her to focus purely on strategy. She describes what she looks for when hiring, then dives into email marketing, ranking audience above offer and copy in driving results and outlining rules for testing, rolling out campaigns, and judging early response speed. - Track a daily break-even number covering all costs including your own salary, and only bank profit once that number is exceeded each day.

## 112. Dr. Sara Shares How To Profit From Amplifying Your Energy, And The "Why, What, How." - I Love Marketing With Joe Polish And Dean Jackson - Episode # 110

Published: 2013-04-09 · [Listen to the original audio](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILoveMarketing110.mp3?dest-id=200948)

Joe Polish and Dean Jackson, joined by Joe's partner Renee Arya, interview Harvard-trained physician Sara Gottfried, author of The Hormone Cure, about the biology behind entrepreneurial overwhelm. She argues that burnout, low energy, and mood problems usually trace back to hormone imbalance, especially excess cortisol, rather than personal failure or purely mental blocks. She names cortisol, estrogen, and thyroid as the key hormones to monitor in women, and cortisol, testosterone, and thyroid in men, and warns that chronic stress accelerates aging, damages memory, and raises disease risk. She offers practical steps including a symptom quiz, a supplement called phosphatidylserine, omega-3 fatty acids, quality sleep, moderating alcohol, and healthy sexual activity to rebalance hormones. The episode frames managing energy as a foundational skill for sustainable business success.

**Key takeaways:**

- Persistent low or inconsistent energy in entrepreneurs is often a hormone imbalance, most commonly excess cortisol, rather than a personal or mental shortcoming.
- Chronic high cortisol from overwork and poor sleep accelerates aging, harms memory and mood, and increases risk of weight gain and disease.
- Simple daily habits such as quality sleep, omega-3 intake, moderate alcohol, and stress-reducing practices can meaningfully rebalance hormones and restore energy.

## 114. The One Where JR Shares His Marketing Insights - I Love Marketing With Joe Polish And Dean Jackson - Episode #113

Joe Polish and Dean Jackson talk with their friend JR, a young but deeply studied marketing strategist who has worked behind the scenes with major authors and thought leaders. JR argues that loving marketing matters both because it brings out a person's best qualities and because it drives income, and he stresses that effective marketing rests equally on hard numbers, such as cost per lead and lifetime customer value, and on a deep understanding of human emotion. Drawing on writers like Eugene Schwartz and Robert Collier, he explains that marketers do not create desire but instead channel existing wants, fears, and status needs already present in an audience. He lists many named human emotions and mental biases as tools for empathizing with customers, and closes with real advertising examples showing how subtle emotional and status cues, not product features, drive buying decisions. The hosts frame this as the philosophical foundation behind their own profit activator framework and plan a follow-up episode to continue the discussion.

**Key takeaways:**

- Effective marketing requires tracking hard numbers such as cost per lead, conversion rates, and lifetime customer value alongside understanding emotion.
- Copy and advertising do not create desire, they channel wants, fears, and status needs that already exist within an audience.
- Naming and recognizing specific human emotions and mental biases helps a marketer empathize with customers and craft messages that truly resonate.

## 115. The One Where Joe, Dean And JR Discuss Human Psychology - I Love Marketing With Joe Polish And Dean Jackson - Episode #114

Published: 2013-05-06 · [Listen to the original audio](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILoveMarketing114.mp3?dest-id=200948)

Joe Polish and Dean Jackson continue their conversation with marketing strategist JR in a third appearance built on the prior episode's discussion of psychology in advertising. Dean shares a real estate example showing how protecting a prospect's ego, rather than shaming them, dramatically increased response to a zero-down-payment home ad. The group explores the difference between compelling someone to respond and later educating and motivating them toward a purchase, using scarcity and urgency at the right stage. JR introduces a checklist of thirty-seven psychological influence techniques drawn from Robert Cialdini's work, and the three discuss examples including higher price signaling higher value, the power of perceived expertise, and social proof, applying each to the hosts' own before, during, and after marketing framework. Joe stresses that ignorance of these principles turns business owners into advertising victims and urges listeners to study human psychology to market ethically and effectively.

**Key takeaways:**

- Advertising that protects a prospect's ego and status works far better than messaging that shames or challenges them into responding.
- Compelling someone to respond, educating and motivating them, and later applying scarcity or urgency are separate stages that should not be rushed together.
- Perceived expertise, higher price, and social proof are psychological shortcuts that make people more likely to trust and buy, and understanding them protects business owners from being sold ineffective advertising.

## 123. The One With 3X Formula Developer Jon Benson - I Love Marketing With Joe Polish And Dean Jackson - Episode #122

Published: 2013-07-24 · [Listen to the original audio](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILoveMarketing122.mp3?dest-id=200948)

Joe Polish and Dean Jackson bring back copywriter John Benson, creator of the video sales letter format, to break down the psychology behind persuasive sales videos. Benson explains his five-step structure: a pattern-interrupting opener, a rapport-building segment using a relatable personal story, revealing the audience's problem is bigger than they realize, presenting a bigger solution, and closing with a benefit-driven offer using future pacing. He introduces modality marketing, matching copy to sensory preferences and to motivations of avoiding pain, seeking enjoyment, and wanting clear instructions. **Key takeaways:**

- A strong sales message follows a repeatable structure: interrupt the audience's pattern, build genuine rapport, expand the problem, then present a bigger solution before the offer.
- People respond to specific sensory language and to messaging framed around avoiding pain, enjoying benefits, and understanding practical steps.
- Improving the sales message itself, rather than chasing more traffic or a bigger brand, is usually the highest-leverage way to grow a business.
