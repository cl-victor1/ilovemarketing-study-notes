## 401. Make Your Mess Your Memoir: How To Transform Your Own Mess Into A Message and Memoir Featuring Anna David, Joe Polish and Dean Jackson - I Love Marketing Episode #375

Published: 2020-06-29 · [Listen to the original episode](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILM_375_-_Anna_David.mp3?dest-id=200948)

Joe Polish and Dean Jackson interview author Anna David about her book Make Your Mess Your Memoir, blending personal story with business lessons. Anna explains a compelling memoir should serve the reader, not the writer, and outlines self-publishing steps, including editors, cover design, and launching with an advance reader team. Dean shares his approach to book titles and treats a first book as a testable pilot rather than a finished masterpiece. Client stories show how a well-targeted book built credibility, referrals, and revenue. The conversation also touches on addiction recovery, since Anna has long been sober, and both hosts point listeners toward online recovery meetings during isolation.

**What I took away:**

- Write your book for what the reader wants to learn, not simply to tell the story you personally want to tell.
- A clear, specific book title that promises a concrete outcome attracts the right readers and can launch or grow a business.
- Treat a first book like a pilot episode you can improve later, rather than delaying release until every detail feels perfect.

## 403. The Art and Craft Of Speaking: The Fourth ILM Zoom Meetup featuring Joel Weldon, Joe Polish, and Dean Jackson - I Love Marketing Episode #377

Published: 2020-07-27 · [Listen to the original episode](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILM_377.mp3?dest-id=200948)

In this recorded Zoom meetup, marketers Joe Polish and Dean Jackson host veteran speaking coach Joel Weldon, a founding member of the National Speakers Association who has coached thousands of presenters through Genius Network. Weldon explains that effective speaking is a learnable skill rooted in authenticity, and that nervousness fades once a speaker focuses on the audience's needs, fears, and past successes rather than self-image. He shares a simple opening structure, explains why a closing story should follow rather than double as the call to action, and gives tips for adapting presentations to virtual settings. Guests Ben Hardy and Jennifer Carroll describe how these methods reshaped their own talks.

**What I took away:**

- Speaking is a learnable skill, and fear fades once a speaker stops focusing on themselves and instead concentrates entirely on meeting the audience's needs, fears, and past successes.
- A strong talk opens by telling listeners what they will know, have, and feel, and keeps the emotional closing story separate from the earlier call to action.
- Virtual presentations need deliberate adjustments, such as dressing as if it were live, removing visual distractions, and stating the agenda early since viewers join and leave at different times.

- Premium pricing can build trust when paired with genuinely superior quality, and unusual, theatrical outreach helps get past gatekeepers to reach real decision makers.

## 405. Boost Your Productivity While Staying Sane: The Fifth ILM Zoom Meetup featuring Eelco de Boer - I Love Marketing Episode #379

Amsterdam-based entrepreneur Eelco de Boer, who runs seven online companies, joins Joe Polish and Dean Jackson to discuss staying productive without burning out. De Boer explains that a truly compelling offer matters more than any marketing tool, and that entrepreneurs should aim to deliver a result so strong they could confidently promise payment only if it works. He describes internet fasting, setting fixed online and offline hours each day to protect focus, plus a simple LinkedIn tactic of driving comments instead of links to boost organic reach. The conversation also covers hiring out low-value tasks to reclaim time and treating money as a solvable goal toward health and happiness.

- Setting fixed daily online and offline hours, called internet fasting, can restore focus, sleep, and creative output for people overwhelmed by constant connectivity.
- On LinkedIn, asking people to comment instead of linking out in a post increases organic reach, since the platform rewards engagement over outbound clicks.

## 406. Designing A Transformative Culture: The I Love Marketing Live Virtual Q&A featuring Jason Korman - I Love Marketing Episode #380

Published: 2020-09-07 · [Listen to the original episode](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILM_380.mp3?dest-id=200948)

Joe Polish and Dean Jackson host Jason Korman, co-founder of Gaping Void Culture Design Group, to explore how organizational culture works as a management system rather than a list of core values. Korman explains that culture change requires three elements: a motivating vision of the future, clearly articulated beliefs that guide daily behavior, and constant reinforcement through visual symbols and repeated language, illustrated with examples from Zappos, the Air Force, and major corporations. He argues that emotion, not data, drives how people act, and that environment strongly shapes behavior. The episode closes with a rapid audience question and answer session covering personal clarity, business syndication, and list segmentation.

**What I took away:**

- Culture functions as a management system built from a shared vision, articulated beliefs, and constant reinforcement, not from posted core values nobody knows how to act on.
- Environment and visual symbols, which Korman calls semiotics, shape people's behavior and emotions more reliably than data or information alone.
- Leaders who communicate a clear, motivating vision of the future, such as Henry Ford or Elon Musk, build stronger cultures and followings than those who rely on facts alone.

## 420. Sleep Transformation: How To Get The Rejuvenating Sleep You Deserve Featuring Dr. Michael Breus and Dean Jackson - I Love Marketing Episode #394

Sleep specialist Dr. Michael Breus joins Dean Jackson to explain why entrepreneurs sleep poorly, especially during the pandemic, and how to fix it. Breus stresses keeping a fixed wake-up time daily based on a person's chronotype, such as early bird or night owl, rather than averaging sleep hours across the week. He covers practical steps including daily movement, cutting off caffeine by early afternoon, stopping alcohol hours before bed, and getting morning sunlight and hydration, plus reframing middle-of-the-night waking as restful rather than stressful. - Stop caffeine by early afternoon and stop drinking alcohol several hours before bed, since both disrupt the deep sleep stage needed for physical recovery.
- If you wake in the middle of the night, stay calm and relax instead of checking the clock, since even quiet rest still provides meaningful recovery.
