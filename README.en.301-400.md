## 301. The Power of Influence and Persuasion with Robert Cialdini - I Love Marketing Episode #278

Published: 2016-10-31 · [Listen to the original audio](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/The_Power_of_Influence_and_Persuasion__Robert_Cialdini.mp3?dest-id=200948)

Joe Polish interviews psychologist Robert Cialdini, author of Influence and the newer book Persuasion, about pre-suasion, the idea that what you say or arrange right before a request matters as much as the request itself. Cialdini reviews his six classic influence principles and explains how a well timed word, question, image, or setting can prime someone toward yes before they ever hear the offer, citing experiments on adventurous framing, romantic settings, website images, background music, and asking for advice instead of opinion. He also shares research showing that organizations relying on dishonest persuasion lose their most ethical employees and retain dishonest ones, while ethical persuasion builds lasting trust.

**Key takeaways:**

- Priming someone's attention or mindset just before delivering a message, called pre-suasion, can dramatically increase how receptive they are to that message.
- Small changes such as asking for advice instead of opinion, or referencing a shared relationship, can shift people toward cooperation and partnership rather than resistance.
- Organizations that use dishonest persuasion tactics tend to lose ethical employees and retain dishonest ones, making ethical influence both more sustainable and more trustworthy long term.

## 302. The Drivers of Desire with Michael Fishman - I Love marketing Episode #279

Published: 2016-11-07 · [Listen to the original audio](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/The_Drivers_of_Desire_Michael_Fishman.mp3?dest-id=200948)

Recorded at a Genius Network event, consumer health and wellness marketing strategist Michael Fishman presents a psychology model called the drivers of desire, explaining that customers experience a product on three levels: what they openly tell, what they privately know but consider too embarrassing to say, and what operates entirely below conscious awareness in the survival driven brain. He illustrates this with weight loss, chronic pain, and a personal story about water in his basement, showing how discomfort often registers unconsciously as a survival threat. He urges marketers to use customers' own private language rather than polished jargon, arguing people buy from those who make them feel truly understood.

**Key takeaways:**

- Customers experience offers on three levels: what they will openly say, what they privately feel but withhold, and unconscious survival level reactions they cannot even name.
- Many pain points, like chronic pain or immobility, unconsciously register in the brain as survival threats even though people describe them in ordinary, practical terms.
- Marketing that uses a customer's own private language, rather than polished industry jargon, builds compassion and trust because people respond most to feeling truly understood.

## 304. Flip Your Flaws with Renee Airya - I Love Marketing Episode #280

Published: 2016-11-14 · [Listen to the original audio](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ilm280.mp3?dest-id=200948)

Recorded at a Genius Network event, former fashion model and coach Renee Airya gives a talk called Flip Your Flaws, describing how a long undiagnosed brain tumor led to emergency surgery at age twenty nine that left her face partly paralyzed. She recounts years of hiding behind her hair before choosing to speak publicly despite her fear, and describes slowly regaining facial movement through daily visualization and exercise. She reframes flaws as gateways to authenticity rather than defects, citing ancient Greek pottery buyers who valued visibly cracked pieces, and offers two steps: viewing a flaw as sincerity, and having an honest conversation about it with a trusted person.

- Hiding a perceived flaw tends to create shame, while openly sharing it can turn that same flaw into a source of authentic connection and personal strength.
- Consistent daily visualization, prayer, and physical practice contributed to unexpected partial recovery of facial movement after a doctor said full paralysis would likely be permanent.
- A short, honest conversation about a hidden flaw with a trusted person can release stuck emotional energy and help someone move from hiding to openness.

## 305. The Lego Principle Can Make You Richer: Marketing Automation Done Right with Brad Martineau - I Love Marketing Episode #281

Published: 2016-11-21 · [Listen to the original audio](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILM_-_281_for_review_1.mp3?dest-id=200948)

Recorded at a Genius Network meeting, automation consultant Brad Martineau, founder of Six Division, argues failed marketing automation projects are rarely the software's fault and almost always a process problem. He introduces the Lego principle, breaking a business into small, separately packaged pieces such as marketing, sales, fulfillment, and financial campaigns, rather than trying to build everything at once. He explains true marketing automation covers far more than autoresponders, including human tasks salespeople and fulfillment staff perform, and recommends a client journey playbook that documents each piece as a clear blueprint before implementation begins. Handing implementers a detailed blueprint, not vague ideas, produces clean campaigns anyone can understand.

**Key takeaways:**

- Automation failures usually stem from a missing or unclear implementation process, not from the software itself.
- Breaking a business into small, separately documented pieces, similar to bagged Lego components, makes complex marketing automation far easier to build and maintain.
- Creating a detailed blueprint of emails, pages, and reports before handing work to an implementer produces cleaner results than describing vague ideas verbally.

## 306. Ideas Worth Using: Creating Results in Advance with Dean Jackson - I Love Marketing Episode #282

Published: 2016-11-29 · [Listen to the original audio](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/Ideas_Worth_Using_Creating_Results_in_Advance__Dean_Jackson.mp3?dest-id=200948)

Recorded at a Genius Network event, podcast co-host Dean Jackson delivers a short talk called Ideas Worth Using, built around one clarifying question: what would you do if you only got paid once your client gets a result. He argues that promising a result before payment requires less proof than convincing someone to pay upfront, since delivering one real result costs less than the sales process needed to win advance payment, and uses his real estate website business as an example. He closes noting people value future money less than money already in hand, and a compelling offer tied to existing self-interest beats any convincing argument.

**Key takeaways:**

- Offering to get paid only after delivering a result can cost less than convincing customers to pay in advance, since delivering one proof case is cheaper than a full sales process.
- People are more generous with money promised in the future than with money already in their possession, making pay-after-result offers easier to accept.
- A compelling offer that taps an existing self-interest is far more persuasive than any argument designed to convince someone of something they do not already want.

## 308. Lessons Learned from a Half Billion Dollars of Product Launches with Jeff Walker - I Love marketing Episode #284

Published: 2016-12-20 · [Listen to the original audio](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/Lessons_Learned_from_a_Half_Billion_Dollars_of_Product_Launches__Jeff_Walker.mp3?dest-id=200948)

Recorded at a Genius Network event, Jeff Walker, creator of the Product Launch Formula and author of the bestseller Launch, shares lessons from launches generating hundreds of millions of dollars. He traces his path from a stay at home father publishing online in 1996 to learning that building relationship and anticipation, much like Apple before a release, beats asking for a sale early. His method has three elements: a multi day sequence of value videos replacing the old sales letter, a story carrying prospects through it, and triggers such as authority and scarcity, illustrated by a nearly free book funnel with a paid upsell that drove a top bestseller ranking.

**Key takeaways:**

- Building anticipation and relationship with an audience before ever asking for a sale, similar to how Apple builds excitement before a product release, drives stronger launch results.
- Replacing a traditional long sales letter with a multi day sequence of story driven value videos builds more buying readiness than a single sales pitch.
- Layering psychological triggers such as authority, reciprocity, and scarcity into a launch sequence, combined with a low priced entry offer and paid upsells, can turn a large volume of buyers into a bestseller.

## 317. Hooked: How to Create Habit-Forming Products with Nir Eyal - I Love Marketing Episode #290

Published: 2017-03-13 · [Listen to the original audio](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/Nir_Eyal_-_How_to_Build_Habit_Forming_Products_AUDIO.mp3?dest-id=200948)

Recorded live at a Genius Network event, author and Stanford lecturer Nir Eyal, known for his book Hooked, explains the design pattern behind habit-forming products like phones and social apps. He breaks the pattern into four steps: a trigger that prompts action, often tied to negative emotions such as boredom or loneliness, a simple action taken in hope of a reward, a variable reward drawn from social approval, search and discovery, or personal mastery, and a final investment step that stores value and pulls the user back for another cycle. He argues most everyday business and government tools fail not because they manipulate people but because they are not engaging enough, and encourages using these same habit techniques ethically to help customers build genuinely useful routines.

**Key takeaways:**

- Habit-forming products rely on a repeating cycle of trigger, action, variable reward, and investment rather than one single clever feature.
- Negative emotions like boredom, loneliness, and uncertainty often act as internal triggers that send people back to certain products or apps.
- Letting users invest effort or data into a product, building stored value like content or reputation, makes them far less likely to switch away.
