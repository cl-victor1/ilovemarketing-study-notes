## 501. How Do You Make CRM Fun: I Love Marketing Live Series with Joe Polish and Dean Jackson - I Love Marketing Episode #475

Published: 2026-06-24 · [Listen to the original episode](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILM_475_How_Do_You_Make_CRM_Fun.mp3?dest-id=200948)

This short episode addresses a listener question about making customer relationship management software more enjoyable to use. Joe Polish admits he leaves database work to his team and focuses instead on marketing strategy, while Dean Jackson offers a framework, treating a database as intelligence for delivering good news rather than tracking cold numbers. Dean illustrates this with a reverse mortgage lender who, instead of pestering real estate agents for attention, built a list of home buyers and could then call agents with an actual interested buyer, turning outreach into a welcome call rather than an unwanted pitch. The lesson is engineering calls so recipients feel genuinely helped, not solicited.

**What I took away:**

- Customer relationship management software becomes more engaging when treated as a tool for gathering intelligence and delivering good news rather than tracking cold numbers.
- Reframing outreach so the other person receives real value, such as an actual buyer lead, turns a sales call into a welcomed conversation.
- Marketing and financial oversight are two areas business owners should stay closely involved in rather than fully delegating to others.

## 502. How Do You Make Auto Repair Fun: I Love Marketing Live Series with Joe Polish and Dean Jackson - I Love Marketing Episode #476

Published: 2026-07-02 · [Listen to the original episode](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILM_476_How_Do_You_Make_Auto_Repair_Fun_I_Love_Marketing_Live_Series_with_Joe_Polish_and_Dean_Jackson.mp3?dest-id=200948)

This brief episode is another clip from a live I Love Marketing question and answer session, answering an auto repair shop owner whose customers expect a small bill but often need far more costly work, making the purchase feel like a grudge rather than something enjoyable. Dean Jackson suggests turning the unexpected expense into an opportunity by bundling extra value into the visit, such as detailing, new accessories, or other perks tied to the repair, so customers leave feeling they gained something. Joe Polish briefly agrees Dean's answer covers it well, and the hosts note how audience chat comments add valuable extra framing during these live recordings.

**What I took away:**

- Turning an unwanted, unexpected expense into a bundle of added value can make a grudge purchase feel more rewarding to the customer.
- Businesses can look for ways to give customers something extra tied to a necessary but unwelcome purchase, improving how the spending feels.
- Recording these episodes live has surfaced valuable audience chat commentary that adds useful framing beyond the hosts' own answers.

## 503. Selling Boring, Confusing, or Depressing: How to Make Marketing Fun I Love Marketing Live Series with Joe Polish and Dean Jackson - I Love Marketing Episode #477

Published: 2026-07-10 · [Listen to the original episode](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILM_477_Selling_Boring_Confusing_or_Depressing_-_How_to_Make_Marketing_Fun_I_Love_Marketing_Live_Series_with_Joe_Polish_and_Dean_Jackson.mp3?dest-id=200948)

This clip from the live question series tackles how to sell something an audience perceives as confusing, boring, or depressing. Joe Polish argues that boredom comes from delivery and framing rather than the underlying product, citing advertising experts who stress selling benefits and destinations rather than dry features or the process itself. Dean Jackson shares his favorite example, a barefoot water skiing training program he relaunched as a playful fictional lotion called Slick Feet 50, complete with a tongue in cheek fake scandal storyline, which made an ordinary instructional video feel exciting and turned the whole promotion into something genuinely fun to create and share with customers.

**What I took away:**

- Selling something perceived as boring works best by focusing on the benefit or destination it delivers, not the dry features or process involved.
- Wrapping an ordinary product in playful storytelling or ritual, as with the fictional Slick Feet 50 lotion, can make purchase and use of it feel exciting.
- Marketers who genuinely believe in what they sell and enjoy creating the promotion tend to produce far more compelling, memorable campaigns.

## 504. Fun Follow Up Sales Tips: The Fun Finale I Love Marketing Live Series with Joe Polish and Dean Jackson - I Love Marketing Episode #478

Published: 2026-07-17 · [Listen to the original episode](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILM_478_Fun_Follow_Up_Sales_Tips_-_The_Fun_Series_Finale_I_Love_Marketing_Live_Series_with_Joe_Polish_and_Dean_Jackson_.mp3?dest-id=200948)

This clip from the live question series covers favorite ways to make sales follow up more enjoyable. Joe Polish and Dean Jackson highlight Derek Sivers and his former company CD Baby, which replaced a routine order confirmation email with a playfully exaggerated story about an imaginary celebration and private jet delivering each customer's compact disc. That single humorous email spread widely online and brought the company many new customers, showing how small creative details can outperform large strategic plans. **What I took away:**

- A single playful, exaggerated follow up email, like the one Derek Sivers wrote for CD Baby, can generate far more word of mouth than a standard generic confirmation.
- Small creative details in customer communication often thrill people more than large, world changing strategic plans.
- Infusing genuine entertainment into an ordinary business, as Jesse Cole did with the Savannah Bananas, can transform its growth and public following.

## 505. Marketing Wins and Roadblocks: The Cap Gap, The Chainsaw, and The Arithmetic Business with Joe Polish and Dean Jackson - I Love Marketing Episode #479

Published: 2026-07-24 · [Listen to the original episode](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILM_479_Marketing_Wins_and_Roadblocks_-_The_Cap_Gap_The_Chainsaw_and_The_Arithmetic_Business_with_Joe_Polish_and_Dean_Jackson_-_I_Love_Marketing_Live_Episode_479.mp3?dest-id=200948)

In this live episode, Joe Polish and Dean Jackson discuss personal marketing wins and roadblocks, then take audience submissions on the same theme. Joe compares adapting to artificial intelligence to a lumberjack receiving a chainsaw, useful only when applied toward real results. Dean introduces his cap gap concept, the gap between what a business could truly deliver and what it currently produces, shown through a hotel executive who replaced a profitable restaurant to capture far larger revenue. Joe recalls lessons from copywriter Gary Halbert about treating marketing as an arithmetic business, then coaches a guest selling hydrogen water on clarifying its real benefit and standing out on crowded shelves.

**What I took away:**

- The cap gap, the space between a business's real capability and its current output, reveals overlooked opportunities for much greater revenue.
- New tools like artificial intelligence only create value when deliberately aimed at concrete business outcomes, similar to a chainsaw only helping once it cuts trees.
- Understanding marketing as measurable arithmetic, tracking numbers and gaps rather than vague feelings, helps identify where a business can meaningfully improve.

## 506. The Results Economy: Getting Out of the Employee Mindset with Joe Polish and Dean Jackson - I Love Marketing Episode #480

Published: 2026-07-31 · [Listen to the original episode](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILM_480_The_Results_Economy_Getting_Out_of_the_Employee_Mindset_-_Joe_Polish_and_Dean_Jackson.mp3?dest-id=200948)

This live episode answers a new entrepreneur named Cliff, who asks how to gain clarity and move past an employee mindset. Dean Jackson advises identifying the single signature result a business delivers and aligning every message with what customers already want, since people focus on their own interests. Joe Polish contrasts the employee mindset, trading time for pay, with an entrepreneurial results economy, where income depends on outcomes, and stresses health, sleep, and energy management over strict scheduling. He references a friend's thinking time practice and Dean's focus finder exercise for clearing mental noise, plus a listener's website business as an example of stating an offering clearly to build trust.

**What I took away:**

- Moving from an employee mindset to an entrepreneurial one means shifting from getting paid for time and effort to getting paid for results and outcomes.
- Aligning your marketing with what customers themselves already want creates a natural pull, since everyone is primarily focused on their own self-interest.
- Clearly identifying and communicating your single signature result helps others immediately understand and trust what you offer, speeding up business growth.

## 507. Cut Through the Noise: Why Your Mom Has a 100% Open Rate with Joe Polish and Dean Jackson - I Love Marketing Episode #481

Published: 2026-08-07 · [Listen to the original episode](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILM_481_Cut_Through_the_Noise_-_Why_Your_Mom_Has_a_Hundred_Percent_Open_Rate_with_Joe_Polish_and_Dean_Jackson.mp3?dest-id=200948)

In another live episode, Joe Polish and Dean Jackson field audience questions on cutting through marketing noise. They open with a listener success story from a dental supplier who applied Joe's what's in it for them philosophy and Dean's nine word email to win business and rank highly in search. The hosts stress marketing must stay ethical, leaving clients satisfied rather than just extracting money. A member named Rocky asks which channels, digital, search, or artificial intelligence, deserve attention given limited time and money. Dean answers by describing attention as scarce, explaining why a personal email inbox, where even a parent's message gets opened instantly, remains the most valuable channel.

**What I took away:**

- Applying proven ideas like the nine word email and what's in it for them consistently, rather than constantly chasing new tactics, drives real marketing results.
- Effective marketing must be ethical, leaving customers satisfied and trusting rather than merely extracting money from them.
- Because human attention is limited and scattered across countless channels, a personal email inbox remains one of the most intimate and effective places to reach people.

## 508. Unique Ability Marketing: How to Stand Out Amid the Noise with Joe Polish and Dean Jackson - I Love Marketing Episode #482

Published: 2026-08-15 · [Listen to the original episode](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILM_482_Unique_Ability_Marketing_How_to_Stand_Out_Amid_the_Noise.mp3?dest-id=200948)

This live episode closes the marketing wins series exploring Dan Sullivan's unique ability concept, an activity performed with energizing skill rather than mere competence. Joe Polish describes his own unique ability as connecting people through interviews and curated groups, while Dean Jackson frames unique ability as the bridge closing the gap between a business's real capability and its results, becoming the core of an offer. Joe introduces an exercise contrasting easy, lucrative, fun activities against hard, annoying ones to identify unique ability. The hosts close on pain as a driver of action, and plans to relaunch local meetup chapters worldwide to keep human connection central despite growing artificial intelligence use.

**What I took away:**

- Unique ability is the activity that energizes rather than drains a person, and clarifying it can become the foundation of an effective marketing offer.
- Sorting activities into easy, lucrative, and fun versus hard, annoying, lame, and frustrating helps identify where a person's true unique ability lies.
- Recognizing and naming a business's pain points, similar to a cap gap, motivates the action needed to close the gap between current and possible results.

## 509. The Wow Package Philosophy: How to Get Anyone's Attention With a Handwritten Card, Pinata, or a Single Shoe with Joe Polish, Dean Jackson, Featuring Tony Jeary - I Love Marketing Episode #483

Published: 2026-08-28 · [Listen to the original episode](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILM_483_The_Wow_Package_Philosophy_How_to_Get_Anyones_Attention_With_a_Handwritten_Card_Pinata_or_a_Single_Shoe_with_Joe_Polish_Dean_Jackson_Featuring_Tony_Jeary.mp3?dest-id=200948)

This episode features Joe Polish and Dean Jackson broadcasting from the Dallas offices of Tony Jeary, a longtime business coach who built a center focused on helping clients get results faster. The conversation centers on standing out through personal, tangible gestures rather than digital noise: handwritten thank-you cards, repurposing old archives into fresh content, and creative attention-getting mailings such as a stamp-filled dollar-store pinata or a single shoe sent to a decision maker to earn a meeting. They cover strategic gifting, the five love languages, calculating return on relationship, and why nurturing existing contacts often beats chasing new leads, closing with a preview of their upcoming live event.

**What I took away:**

- Personal, tangible outreach such as handwritten cards or creative packages cuts through digital noise far more effectively than email or social media.
- Repurposing existing archives and materials, such as old magazines or member books, can create a long stream of valuable, low-cost marketing content.
- Nurturing and appreciating existing relationships, measured through a metric like return on relationship, generates more value than constantly chasing new customers.

## 510. The 8 Profit Activators in Action: Live Hot Seats With Joe Polish and Dean Jackson - I Love Marketing Episode #484

Published: 2026-09-18 · [Listen to the original episode](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILM_484_The_8_Profit_Activators_Live_Real_Time_Hot_Seats_With_Joe_Polish_and_Dean_Jackson.mp3?dest-id=200948)

Joe Polish and Dean Jackson, joined by host Paul Colligan, revisit the origin and structure of their eight Profit Activators framework, tracing it back to Dean's marketing journals from the nineteen nineties. They briefly explain each activator, covering target market selection, compelling offers, patient education, easy-to-start service packages, dream outcomes, after-sale follow-up, lifetime relationships, and orchestrated referrals. The bulk of the episode is live hot-seat coaching with audience members: a luxury powerboat racing league founder pricing multimillion-dollar national team licenses, a coach discussing referrals for high-ticket clients, an author worried about ego barriers to selling books, and a painting contractor exploring how his personal story could become his marketing message.

**What I took away:**

- The eight Profit Activators framework moves prospects through selecting a target market, making compelling offers, educating patiently, delivering an ideal experience, and orchestrating referrals.
- Consistent daily action, like sending handwritten notes or building a new habit, compounds into meaningful business and personal results over time.
- Effective marketing identifies what a customer truly wants behind a product, such as status or a desired outcome, rather than promoting the product itself.
