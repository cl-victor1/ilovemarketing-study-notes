## 201. Yellow Pages Roulette and A Four Step Method For Writing Compelling Ads

Published: 2015-02-09 · [Listen to the original audio](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILoveMarketing187.mp3?dest-id=200948)

Joe Polish and Dean Jackson play their recurring Yellow Pages Roulette game, opening a phone directory at random pages to critique real advertisements using their eight profit activator framework. They analyze a bankruptcy attorney ad, explaining how to reach embarrassed, invisible prospects through a non-threatening free recorded message that educates rather than pressures. They then revisit a dentist ad case study, showing how narrowing a full page ad down to a small, pain focused headline dramatically increased response by targeting the single most likely searcher. Along the way they discuss writing enthusiastic, benefit driven copy for mundane products like tweezers, and the difference between visible and invisible prospects.

**Key takeaways:**

- Ads should target the single most urgent prospect, such as someone in dental pain, rather than trying to appeal broadly with generic feature lists.
- Offering education through a free recorded message or similar non-threatening tool helps convert embarrassed or invisible prospects who are not ready to buy immediately.
- Enthusiastic, benefit focused copy can make even an ordinary product, like tweezers, feel compelling and worth choosing over competitors.

## 202. Darren Hardy Presents 100 Years of Wisdom In 10 Minutes

Published: 2015-02-16 · [Listen to the original audio](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILoveMarketing188.mp3?dest-id=200948)

Darren Hardy, publisher of Success magazine, addresses a Genius Network audience about the most influential person he has interviewed, a self-made centenarian immigrant who built vast wealth without ever attending high school. Hardy recounts traveling to interview the man, expecting pages of wisdom but receiving a mostly blank page whose central idea was that only a few things truly matter in any pursuit. He connects this to Dr. Oz focusing on the few surgical steps that matter most, Steve Jobs defining focus as saying no to good ideas, and Warren Buffett rejecting nearly all opportunities offered him. Hardy urges listeners to master those few vital actions instead of chasing novelty.

**Key takeaways:**

- Long term success usually comes down to identifying a small number of vital actions and mastering them rather than constantly chasing new ideas.
- True focus means deliberately saying no to many good opportunities so effort concentrates on what matters most.
- Even highly accomplished people, like a heart surgeon or a top investor, credit their success to doing a few critical things extremely well.

## 203. Marc Goodman, Author of Future Crimes On How To Protect Yourself From Crime Online

Published: 2015-02-23 · [Listen to the original audio](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILoveMarketing189_MGoodman.mp3?dest-id=200948)

Joe Polish talks with Marc Goodman, a former police officer and futurist who advised the Federal Bureau of Investigation and Interpol, about the hidden dangers of connected technology, drawn from his book about future crimes. Goodman explains how hacking has grown from a hobby into an organized global business, how criminals exploit social media oversharing, wearable devices, implanted medical devices, self-driving cars, and even robots, and how terrorists now use drones and encrypted communication. He also describes how cryptocurrency enables anonymous crime and large-scale theft. The conversation closes with a memorable framework for everyday protection covering software updates, strong unique passwords, careful downloads, limited administrator access, and encryption.

**Key takeaways:**

- Cybercrime has become an automated, scalable business that can victimize millions of people at once instead of one person at a time.
- Everyday devices such as fitness trackers, medical implants, cars, and smart home gadgets can be hacked because they are all connected computers with weak security.
- Following simple habits like updating software, using long unique passwords, avoiding administrator accounts, and encrypting data can prevent most common cyberattacks.

## 205. Proven Human Behavior "Hot Buttons" That Influence and Persuade People To Buy From You

Published: 2015-03-02 · [Listen to the original audio](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILoveMarketing190.mp3?dest-id=200948)

Joe Polish and Dean Jackson have a freewheeling conversation working through a list of human behavior and persuasion notes originally compiled by a direct response marketer friend. They cover ideas such as awakening desires people already have rather than trying to create new ones, the importance of narrowing focus to a well chosen audience instead of chasing broad reach, tying offers to existing beliefs rather than arguing against them, and using influence tactics like scarcity, takeaway, and social proof ethically. They also discuss why buying does not satisfy desire but excites it, making upsells and ongoing offers essential, and why revisiting older marketing and psychology books often reveals timeless lessons.

**Key takeaways:**

- Effective marketing awakens a desire that already exists in the audience rather than trying to manufacture a new one.
- Choosing a narrow, well-defined audience and meeting people where their current beliefs are works better than mass reach or direct argument.
- A completed purchase excites further buying rather than satisfying it, which is why upsells, follow-up offers, and ongoing relationships matter.

## 207. John Ratliff On How You Can Create A Highly Productive Company Culture

Published: 2015-03-09 · [Listen to the original audio](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILovemarketing191.mp3?dest-id=200948)

This episode features a talk by John Ratliff, founder of a large answering service company he built and later sold, recorded at a Genius Network event. Ratliff argues that common excuses about weak work ethic and a lack of good candidates are outdated thinking, and that a genuine employee-first culture is a real competitive advantage. He describes how a wake-up call about extremely high frontline turnover led him to overhaul his company's culture, dramatically cutting turnover and multiplying the company's value. He warns that surface-level perks mean nothing without sincere philosophy behind them, and shares practical steps such as core values, feedback surveys, and town hall meetings.

**Key takeaways:**

- Blaming poor company culture on employees or a lack of good candidates is usually a leadership excuse, not the real problem.
- Superficial perks like games or motivational posters fail, and can even backfire, unless paired with a genuine philosophy of respecting employees.
- Leaders do not need to personally like every employee, but they owe them respect and fair treatment because employees drive the business forward.

## 208. Best Selling Author Lisa Sasevich Interview About Her New Book, Boost Your Sales: How To Use Irresistible Offers Without Being Salesy

Published: 2015-03-11 · [Listen to the original audio](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILoveMarketing192.mp3?dest-id=200948)

Joe Polish and Dean Jackson interview Lisa Sasevich, known for teaching sales conversion to heart-centered entrepreneurs, about her newly released book on making irresistible offers without being pushy. Sasevich explains why failing to make a clear offer after educating an audience is a disservice, since prospects either lose momentum or buy from a competitor instead. She describes her method for uncovering a compelling transformation by focusing on one ideal client, then breaks an irresistible offer into three parts: a clear main deliverable, a few tightly related bonuses, and limiters that create healthy urgency. She shares real examples, including a teacher who earned thousands of dollars from one presentation.

**Key takeaways:**

- Educating or inspiring an audience without making a clear offer is a disservice, because interested prospects often buy from a competitor instead.
- Clarifying a specific transformation for one ideal client, rather than a general audience, makes it much easier to describe an offer persuasively.
- A strong offer needs a clear main deliverable, a few closely related bonuses, and a genuine time or quantity limiter to prompt people to decide now.

## 209. Jamie Smart And The Truth About Feelings: How To Reduce Worry, Overwhelm Or Stress

Published: 2015-03-16 · [Listen to the original audio](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILM193_JamieSmart_part1.mp3?dest-id=200948)

- Feelings come from thought occurring in the present moment, not directly from outside circumstances, even though it strongly feels otherwise.
- Recognizing this misunderstanding about the source of feelings can quickly dissolve stress and overwhelm and reveal solutions that were already obvious.
- Real clarity and insight often arise when people fall out of their habitual, repetitive thinking rather than by trying harder to solve a problem.

## 210. Jamie Smart And The Truth About Feelings: How To Reduce Worry, Overwhelm Or Stress

## 214. Two Proven Phrases To Increase Your Conversions And Transform Your Business - I Love Marketing Episode #198

Published: 2015-04-20 · [Listen to the original audio](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILoveMarketing198.mp3?dest-id=200948)

Joe Polish and Dean Jackson interview longtime direct marketing and health industry consultant Michael Fishman about the psychology behind consumer behavior and conversion. Fishman explains why understanding how someone arrived on a list, such as through a sweepstakes versus valuable content, predicts how committed and loyal they will be, and distinguishes opportunistic responses from committed ones. He describes the difference between personal brands and humanized company brands, and shares practical phrasing advice, such as writing opt-in buttons in the visitor's own voice rather than giving commands, since people resist being told what to do even when they want the offer.

**Key takeaways:**

- How a person joined a list or subscribed, whether through a quick incentive or genuine interest, predicts their long-term loyalty and value as a customer.
- Writing calls to action in the visitor's own voice, rather than as a command, increases conversions by avoiding instinctive resistance to being told what to do.
- Businesses can build trust either through a personal brand centered on one person or by humanizing a company brand, and each approach has different long-term implications for growth and eventual sale of the business.

## 217. How To Build, Protect and Pass On Your Wealth With Myra Salzer - I Love Marketing With Joe Polish and Dean Jackson Episode #200

Published: 2015-05-04 · [Listen to the original audio](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILoveMarketing200.mp3?dest-id=200948)

Joe Polish interviews Myra Salzer, founder of Wealth Conservancy, about coaching families and individuals who inherit significant wealth. She contrasts wealth creators with inheritors, noting that confidence often separates the two, since heirs rarely experience failure and self-reliance the way builders of wealth do. Salzer discusses stereotypes about rich people, the isolation and grief that can follow a sudden inheritance, and the importance of letting children fail so they gain real-world competence. She recommends avoiding impulsive decisions after receiving money, distinguishes true philanthropy from guilt-driven giving, and shares stories illustrating anonymity, confidence, and family dynamics among wealthy clients. - Confidence, built through experiencing failure and recovering from it, is what most separates wealth creators from those who inherit money.
- New heirs should avoid major decisions right after a windfall and instead allow themselves time to process the change before acting.
- Genuine philanthropy comes from personal involvement and intention rather than simply writing a check out of guilt or obligation.

## 219. Brian Kurtz and Joe Polish On How To Hire A Marketing Consultant - I Love Marketing Episode #202
