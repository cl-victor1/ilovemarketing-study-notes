## 1. The One Where We Start At The Beginning - I Love Marketing With Joe Polish And Dean Jackson - Episode #1

Published: 2010-01-16 · [Listen to the original audio](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/I_love_Marketing_01.mp3?dest-id=200948)

This launch episode introduces the show, hosted by direct response marketers Dean Jackson and Joe Polish. They explain their goal: to record their ongoing conversations about marketing so listeners can benefit from decades of combined experience. Both hosts trace their earliest exposure to marketing and selling, from childhood jobs and door-to-door sales to Dean's creation of one of the first email opt-in landing pages for a real estate website in 1997. They introduce foundational concepts including the push, pull, and dangle model for online marketing, Dean's eyeballs plus emails plus hearts formula for turning website visitors into real relationships, and the importance of focusing marketing efforts on a narrow, well-defined target market rather than trying to appeal to everyone. The episode closes by teasing Joe's story about discovering direct response marketing through a chance conversation at a lake.

**Key takeaways:**

- Effective online marketing can be broken into three functions: pulling visitors to a site, dangling something valuable to capture contact information, and pushing ongoing communication to them afterward.
- Reducing a webpage to a single possible action, such as leaving an email address, increases the number of visitors who take that action.
- Businesses grow faster by picking a narrow target market and dominating it rather than trying to serve everyone.
- Marketing knowledge learned in one business, such as real estate or carpet cleaning, transfers easily to other industries once the underlying principles are understood.

## 2. The One Where Joe Grows His Business 500% - I Love Marketing With Joe Polish And Dean Jackson - Episode #2

Published: 2011-01-23 · [Listen to the original audio](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/i_love_marketing_02.mp3?dest-id=200948)

Joe Polish continues the origin story from the previous episode, describing how a wealthy acquaintance's blunt advice at a lake convinced him to master fundamental business skills rather than switch industries. Struggling financially with his carpet cleaning company, Joe read Michael Gerber's book about systemizing a business, then discovered direct response marketing through a Gary Halbert newsletter. He explains how he learned education-based marketing, hired a copywriter to produce a consumer awareness guide that addressed customer misconceptions and questions, paired it with a free recorded phone message, and applied ideas from Gary Halbert and consultant Jay Abraham, including the concept of a customer's lifetime value. By shifting from a cheap, price-based pitch to an informative, trust-building approach, Joe grew his carpet cleaning company's monthly revenue from about $2,100 to over $12,000 within six months, a change that eventually led him toward a career as a marketing consultant and trainer.

**Key takeaways:**

- Learning fundamental business and marketing skills matters more than switching to a new industry when a business is not profitable.
- An education-based consumer guide that answers customer questions and misconceptions builds trust and reduces price-based buying decisions.
- Good marketing does the selling in advance, so that by the time a salesperson talks to a prospect, that prospect is already interested and ready to buy.
- Understanding a customer's lifetime value justifies spending more to acquire and impress each new customer.
- Joe grew his carpet cleaning company's monthly revenue roughly six-fold within six months simply by changing his marketing message, not his technical skills.

## 3. The One Where Dean Gets Off The Hamster Wheel - I Love Marketing With Joe Polish And Dean Jackson - Episode #3

Published: 2011-01-31 · [Listen to the original audio](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/003ILoveMarketing.mp3?dest-id=200948)

Dean Jackson shares his own origin story, describing how reading Michael Gerber's book about building a business that works without the owner inspired him to move away from labor-intensive cold calling toward creating reusable marketing systems for his real estate business. He describes producing a homebuyer guide and companion seminar, then turning that content into a low-cost cable television show, and mailing postcards that invited apartment renters to request the guide by mail. These systems freed him from what he calls the hamster wheel of constant one-on-one prospecting and let him instead build what he calls an oil well, a marketing asset that keeps generating qualified leads with little ongoing effort. Dean and Joe compare their parallel discoveries of spending money on marketing rather than only spending time and energy, and stress that a modest, properly targeted investment in direct response marketing usually returns far more than typical investments.

**Key takeaways:**

- A marketing system built once, such as a guide, television segment, or mailed postcard, can keep generating leads repeatedly with little further effort, unlike one-to-one prospecting.
- Dean compares constant person-to-person selling to a hamster wheel and a reusable lead-generating system to an oil well that keeps producing after the initial setup effort.
- Spending money on tested, trackable direct response marketing usually produces a much higher return than most other investments.
- Building leverage requires investing time upfront on something that will not pay off immediately, before it can work automatically afterward.

## 4. The One Where It's Your Turn To Try It - I Love Marketing With Joe Polish And Dean Jackson - Episode #4

Published: 2011-02-06 · [Listen to the original audio](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILoveMarketing004.mp3?dest-id=200948)

Joe Polish and Dean Jackson break down the mechanics of education-based marketing, the approach both used in their earliest successful campaigns. Drawing on an idea from an old copywriting book that an advertisement should enter the conversation already happening inside a prospect's mind, they explain how to shift promotional material away from a business's own name and logo and toward the questions and concerns of the target customer. Joe walks through the structure of his carpet cleaning consumer guide, covering headlines about avoiding rip-offs, common misconceptions, and guarantees, and shows how the same template applies to any industry, including dentistry and Dean's example of a barefoot water skiing school. They also discuss building an ongoing newsletter to keep communicating with prospects after they request the initial guide, since different products carry different buying cycles, and they set up the next episode's topic of converting those leads into appointments and sales.

**Key takeaways:**

- Effective marketing material should address a prospect's existing worries and questions rather than promote the business's name, logo, or image.
- A consumer awareness guide follows a repeatable template covering common rip-offs, misconceptions, mistakes to avoid, and a guarantee, and this template can be adapted to almost any industry.
- After a prospect requests the initial guide, an ongoing newsletter or update series keeps the relationship warm until the prospect is ready to buy.
- The best follow-up content often comes from directly asking customers what frustrates them or what questions they commonly have.

## 5. The One About Converting Leads - I Love Marketing With Joe Polish And Dean Jackson - Episode #5

Published: 2011-02-14 · [Listen to the original audio](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILoveMarketing005.mp3?dest-id=200948)

Joe Polish and Dean Jackson explain how to convert leads from consumer awareness offers into paying customers. Joe describes his carpet cleaning system of a free room of cleaning paired with a home carpet inspection, showing each marketing step should move a prospect toward the next step like dominoes falling. Dean contrasts merely being available to prospects with actively making them a specific offer, using an analogy about handing someone a fresh cookie instead of pointing them toward the refrigerator. They cover reciprocity, planning a sales sequence in advance, and data showing most buyers purchase well after their first inquiry, so long-term follow-up captures most of a lead list's value.

**Key takeaways:**

- A marketing sequence works best when every step is designed to accomplish one goal, moving the prospect forward rather than trying to close the sale too early.
- Making a specific, concrete offer converts far better than simply telling prospects you are available, because people want to be led rather than left to decide on their own.
- Since most inquiries do not turn into a sale within the first ninety days, consistent long-term follow-up with a full portfolio of leads captures most of their eventual value.

## 6. The One About Converting Even More Leads - I Love Marketing With Joe Polish And Dean Jackson - Episode #6

Published: 2011-02-22 · [Listen to the original audio](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILoveMarketing006.mp3?dest-id=200948)

Joe Polish and Dean Jackson continue their talk on turning leads into sales. Joe tells how he advised supplement entrepreneur Bill Phillips to remail one successful sales letter twice more with handwritten second and final notices, bringing in millions in extra revenue at almost no added cost. They argue businesses should communicate as often as they can add value, since customers who disappear usually leave from neglect rather than too much contact. They weigh newsletter mailing costs against long-term customer profit to show frequent outreach nearly always pays off, then close by defining a five-star prospect and retelling Gary Halbert's hamburger stand story about finding a hungry, ready-to-buy audience.

**Key takeaways:**

- Remailing the same successful sales piece with a handwritten second and third notice can generate large additional revenue for very little extra cost.
- Businesses rarely lose customers from contacting them too often, since customers who quietly leave usually do so because of neglect rather than too much communication.
- Defining an ideal five-star prospect, someone engaged, ready to act soon, and open to help, and focusing follow-up on that group beats treating every lead the same.

## 7. The One Where We Play Yellow Pages Roulette - I Love Marketing With Joe Polish And Dean Jackson - Episode #7

Published: 2011-02-28 · [Listen to the original audio](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILoveMarketing007.mp3?dest-id=200948)

Joe Polish and Dean Jackson pick random pages from a phone directory and brainstorm how to market whatever business turns up, landing on roofing and then aquariums. For roofing, they map a neighborhood of homes nearing the end of a roof's lifespan, calculate that market's total value, and use small mailings to build trust before a costly repair is needed. For aquariums, Dean suggests placing tanks in pediatrician and dentist waiting rooms to reach parents through their children. Throughout, they stress identifying a specific target market, calculating its long-term value, and using low pressure education rather than generic advertising to earn attention.

**Key takeaways:**

- Estimating the total future value of a well-defined target market, such as a neighborhood of homes due for the same repair, shows how much a business can profitably spend to win that business early.
- Small, inexpensive, ongoing contact with a likely future customer builds trust long before a large purchase is needed, so the business is chosen automatically when the need finally arises.
- Placing a product or demonstration where an ideal audience already gathers, such as aquariums in waiting rooms full of parents, can generate interest without traditional advertising.

- Every marketing piece, whether an advertisement, a recorded message, or a guide, needs one specific next step for the reader to take, or the effort spent educating people will not convert into sales.

## 10. The One With Gary Vaynerchuk - I Love Marketing With Joe Polish And Dean Jackson - Episode #10

Published: 2011-03-21 · [Listen to the original audio](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILoveMarketing010.mp3?dest-id=200948)

Joe Polish and Dean Jackson welcome their first guest, entrepreneur and author Gary Vaynerchuk, who grew a small family wine business into a large company through early adoption of internet and social media marketing. Gary explains the core idea of his book, that context now matters more than content, and argues businesses must shift from one-way, push style advertising toward ongoing, personal conversation with customers on social platforms. He predicts traditional conversion tactics will keep weakening and warns that businesses ignoring this shift will struggle within a few years. - Gary Vaynerchuk argues that context, the ongoing relationship a business builds with its audience, now matters more for winning customers than content or traditional advertising alone.
- Businesses should treat social media as a continuous conversation rather than a one-time campaign, responding personally instead of only pushing out offers and messages.
- Because reputations are now fully visible online, careless or unethical business practices get exposed quickly, making genuine care for customers a long-term advantage rather than optional.

## 15. The One Where You Can't Help But Make More Money Just By Listening - I Love Marketing With Joe Polish And Dean Jackson - Episode #15

They break the framework into eight steps, including choosing a single target market, compelling prospects to make contact, educating and motivating them to meet, delivering a distinctive and memorable experience, and later nurturing repeat business and orchestrating referrals. ## 16. The One With Dan Kennedy - I Love Marketing With Joe Polish And Dean Jackson - Episode #16

Joe Polish and Dean Jackson interview veteran direct response marketer Dan Kennedy, who traces his path from traditional advertising into direct marketing and explains why claims that direct mail, long copy, or older media are dead tend to be false. Kennedy argues that marketing is the true engine of every business regardless of its product, that the internet is simply another medium with its own strengths and risks, and that relying on only one marketing channel is dangerous because any medium can disappear overnight. - Relying on a single marketing channel is risky because any medium, no matter how profitable, can be regulated away or disappear almost overnight.

## 21. The One About Naming (From The Car) - I Love Marketing With Joe Polish And Dean Jackson - Episode #21

## 23. The One With Tellman Knudson (OvercomeEverything.com) - I Love Marketing With Joe Polish And Dean Jackson - Episode #23

Published: 2011-06-20 · [Listen to the original audio](https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/traffic.libsyn.com/secure/ilovemarketing/ILoveMarketing23.mp3?dest-id=200948)

Joe Polish and Dean Jackson talk with Tellman Knudson, founder of Overcome Everything, a hypnotherapist turned email marketing specialist known for extreme goals like running barefoot across the country for a youth homelessness charity. Knudson explains why email is the cheapest, most measurable way to reach an audience, and why building a list should be a top priority. He covers capturing names both online and offline, a rough guideline of a dollar of monthly revenue per subscriber, and why marketing means genuinely helping people. He also shares a trick for writing subject lines that get opened by borrowing language from widely forwarded personal emails, and recommends autoresponder tools for newcomers.

**Key takeaways:**

- Building an email list should be a daily top priority because it is cheap, trackable, and profitable compared to other channels.
